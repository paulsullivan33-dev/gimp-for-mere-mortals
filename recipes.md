# Recipes

Short jobs, whole. Each recipe names
its tools by chapter, assumes you start
from a copy with the original untouched
(chapter 1's rule), and ends with the
check that tells you it worked.

## Rescue a grey-day photograph

*Levels, a whisper of saturation.*
Duplicate the layer (chapter 5). Open
**Colors → Levels** and set the black
and white triangles to the feet of the
histogram's mountains, middle triangle
to taste (chapter 7). If the colours
still look washed, **Colors →
Hue-Saturation** and raise Saturation
by ten, no more. Toggle the layer's
eye to compare. Check: real blacks,
real whites, and grass that is green
without being neon.

## Brighten the sky without touching the ground

*A selection, then levels inside it.*
Wand-select the sky (**Fuzzy Select**,
chapter 6), **Select → Feather** by a
few pixels so the horizon will not
show a seam, then **Colors → Levels**
and lift the middle triangle. The
fence keeps every adjustment in the
sky; the ground keeps its own light.
**Select → None** when done. Check:
zoom into the horizon line — if you
can see where the edit stops, feather
more and redo.

## Take the scratch out of a scan

*Heal, small strokes, honest limits.*
Zoom until the scratch fills the
screen. With the **Heal** tool
(chapter 7), **Ctrl**-click clean
texture beside the scratch, then paint
along it in short strokes,
re-sourcing whenever the background
changes — sky texture for sky, grass
for grass. Check: at normal zoom the
scratch is gone and no smear repeats
a pattern the eye can catch. (The
lab's sample image carries a scratch
drawn in on purpose, so its batch
chapters had honest damage to
contemplate.)

## Watermark a set of pictures

*A text layer, turned down low.*
Open the picture, add a text layer
with your name or your studio's
(chapter 9), set its **Opacity** in
the Layers dialog to about 25, and
place it where removing it would ruin
the picture — across the middle beats
a corner that crops away. Save the
XCF *without* the watermark as your
negative, then export the watermarked
copy. For a folder of them, chapter
10's loop is the same recipe with the
human removed. Check: the mark is
legible when you look for it and
ignorable when you don't.

## A banner from a photograph

*Crop to shape, then scale to size.*
**Crop** tool, **Fixed aspect ratio**
set to the banner's proportions —
3:1 is common — and frame the part of
the photo that tells the story
(chapter 8). Then **Image → Scale
Image** to the exact pixel width the
page wants, a whisper of sharpening,
and export as JPEG at quality 85.
Check: the exported file's dimensions
in your file manager are the numbers
you were asked for, to the pixel.

## Melt two photographs into one

*A gradient on a mask.* Stack the two
photos as layers, the newcomer on
top. Add a **layer mask** to the top
layer (chapter 6), choose the
**Gradient** tool, and drag a
black-to-white gradient across the
mask where the pictures should meet.
The top photo fades out along the
gradient and the bottom one takes
over, seamlessly, and the seam's
place and softness are yours to move
by re-dragging. Check: no edge is
visible at any zoom — only a
transition.
